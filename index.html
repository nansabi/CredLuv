forensic capabilities

Two ways of reading a document that catch different things.

No single technique catches every forgery. CREDLUV layers texture-level and shape-level analysis so each covers the other's blind spots.

DCT — texture fingerprinting

Breaks the document into blocks and compares their compression pattern. A pasted-in region carries a different texture signature than its surroundings, even when it looks identical to the eye.

SIFT / A-KAZE — landmark matching

Marks distinct landmarks — a signature's curve, a seal's edge — and checks whether the same landmark appears twice. Stays reliable even if the document is rotated, resized, or photographed at an angle.

OCR + spatial verification

Reads every field, then checks it sits where a genuine document of this type would expect it — catching layout drift a forger overlooked.

Few-shot fine-tuning

Learns an institution's genuine format from as few as 10 samples using background regularization — practical across thousands of colleges and offices without massive datasets.

why it holds up

What makes this architecture hard to fool.

Active forensic defense

Continuously scrubs for the kind of invisible forgeries — ink variance, texture seams, pixel-level splicing — that a passive, signature-only check would never see.

Works without cooperation

Doesn't require the issuing institution to have joined any platform. A 15-year-old paper certificate from a college that no longer exists can still be examined.

Explainable, not a black box

Every flagged result comes with a heatmap pointing to the exact region and reason — evidence a human reviewer can verify in seconds, not a score they have to trust blindly.

Privacy by design

Only a cryptographic hash is stored long-term. Personal data lives in a deletable database, kept DPDP Act–compliant, with zero-knowledge proofs on the roadmap for selective disclosure.

Gets smarter with use

Fraud patterns caught anywhere in the network improve detection everywhere else, through privacy-preserving federated updates — model weights only, never raw documents.

what we're honest about

A first-pass filter, not a final verdict.

A system that claims to be infallible can't be trusted in a legal context. Here's where CREDLUV knows its limits — and how it accounts for them.

—

With limited training data, real-world accuracy runs closer to 50–65%, not the 90%+ figures seen in general benchmarks. Few-shot learning narrows this gap over time, not overnight.

—

A cleanly forged document with zero editing artifacts can still slip past forensic analysis alone — the fraud registry check exists as a second line of defense.

—

No master database of every authentic document in India exists yet. Coverage grows institution by institution, starting with pilot partners.

—

A confidence score is never treated as a legal verdict on its own — every flagged case requires a human reviewer's sign-off.
